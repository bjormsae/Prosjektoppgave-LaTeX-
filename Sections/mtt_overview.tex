\section{Multi-Target Tracking: An overview}
\label{sec:mtt-overview}

% ---- Opening + reading guide --------------------------------------
Multi-target tracking (MTT) is the problem of estimating the number of
targets and their states over time from noisy, incomplete and
ambiguous sensor data. This section surveys the field in six steps,
each answering one question. Table~\ref{tab:challenges} asks
\emph{why the problem is hard}; Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} asks
\emph{what the parts of a tracker are}; Fig.~\ref{fig:taxonomy} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage} give a \emph{map} of the approaches and how they
evolved; Section~\ref{sec:families} describes each family;
Section~\ref{sec:compare} compares them through three lenses
(Tables~\ref{tab:compare}--\ref{tab:stress}, Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff});
and Fig.~\ref{fig:selection} closes with a selection guide.
Colours identify method families consistently throughout.

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $X_k=\{\mathbf{x}_k^{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_k^{N_k}\}$ be the set of
target states at time $k$, with $N_k$ unknown and time-varying, and
$Z_k$ the set of measurements received. Each target evolves as
$\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=f(\mathbf{x}_k)+\mathbf{w}_k$, and is detected with
probability $\PD$, producing $\mathbf{z}_k=h(\mathbf{x}_k)+\mathbf{v}_k$.
The set $Z_k$ additionally contains clutter, and the origin of each
measurement is unknown. \TODO{Fix notation here once and use it everywhere,
including the RFS families later.}

\paragraph{Running example (Scenario S).}
Two vessels on crossing courses, a third vessel joining the area,
intermittent detections (varying sea state, sensor gaps) and sea-return
clutter. Scenario~S is revisited in Section~\ref{sec:compare} to show
where each family struggles.
